Judgy How it judges FAQ

Privacy

Last updated: August 2026

Judgy's whole job is judging the page you ask it about. That requires sending some data to our server. This page says exactly what, when, and for how long — in plain English, because a privacy policy you can't read isn't one.

When you click the button

Clicking Judgy sends the current page's visible content (title, description, body text, reviews) and its URL to Judgy's server, which passes the content to Anthropic's Claude models to produce a score.

  • The page content is used for the rating and not stored. Our database keeps only the URL, the score, and the written explanation.
  • Tracking parameters (like utm_ tags) are stripped from URLs before anything is stored.

While you browse (the toolbar icon)

So the icon can show a score on pages that have already been judged, Judgy checks visited page URLs against the existing score cache. This check sends only the URL — never the page content — and it never creates a new rating. Unmatched URLs are not recorded.

What we store, and for how long

  • Scores and explanations, keyed by cleaned URL. Product and general-page ratings expire after about 90 days; article ratings after about 180 days; ratings of frozen content (like a specific post) are kept so repeat visits stay instant.
  • That's the whole database. No accounts, no browsing history, no profiles of you.

What we never do

  • No ads, no analytics trackers, no selling or sharing data with anyone except Anthropic, whose models produce the ratings.
  • No reading pages you didn't ask about — page content leaves your browser only on your click.
  • No affiliate links. Judgy has no financial stake in what it scores, which is the entire point.

Third parties

Page content submitted for rating is processed by Anthropic under their privacy policy. That is the only third party involved.

Questions

Fair questions, honest answers.

What does Judgy actually rate?

The thing the page is about — the product, the article, the post — never the page's design, its photography, or its price. A beautiful listing for a mediocre product gets a mediocre score.

Why did it give my favorite thing a 6?

Because 6 means "genuinely above average," and most good things are exactly that. Judgy holds the top of the scale for the exceptional, so the numbers keep meaning something.

What won't it rate?

Paywalled articles it can't actually read, private people's profile pages, and browser-internal pages. If Judgy can't see the real thing, it declines rather than guessing.

What data leaves my browser?

Page content is only sent when you click the button. In the background, Judgy checks visited URLs (just the address, never the content) against already-issued scores so the toolbar icon can light up. No accounts, no ads, no selling data. Full details in the privacy policy.

Is it free?

Yes — every day you get a batch of free judgments, and pages that have already been judged never count against it. Every fresh judgment costs us a model call, so the daily cap keeps the lights on.
